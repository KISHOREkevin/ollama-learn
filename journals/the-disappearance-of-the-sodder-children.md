# **The Disappearance of the Sodder Children**

# **Kentucky, USA**

## **The Beginning**

On Christmas Eve in 1945, a fire destroyed the Sodder family home in Fayetteville, West Virginia. Five of the nine Sodder children were missing, but no bodies were found. The family's home was badly damaged, but no signs of forced entry or exit were detected.

## **What Happened**

On Christmas morning, the Sodder children woke up to find their father, Lewis Sodder, huddled in a corner with the children. Their mother, Nancy, was missing. The children, including Maurice, Thelma, Louis, Jennie, and Betty, were shaken but unharmed.

## **The Investigation**

The police investigation revealed that the fire had destroyed the house in a matter of minutes, but no signs of a fire or arson were found. The fire had been extinguished before it spread to the house. The police also found no evidence of forced entry or exit.

## **The Evidence**

* A coal bale in the fireplace had been ripped open, suggesting that someone had entered through it.
* A piece of torn fabric was found on the floor near the fireplace.
* The fire had damaged several rooms in the house, but no signs of a struggle or trauma were found.
* The police collected fingerprints from the fireplace and the coal bale, which were later matched to an unknown individual.

## **The Aftermath**

The police investigation was unable to determine who was responsible for the disappearance of the Sodder children. The case remained a mystery for many years, but in 2011, new DNA evidence was collected from the coal bale and matched to a 1960s-era DNA sample that had been previously thought to be extinct.

## **The Aftermath**

On December 12, 2011, the remains of the Sodder children were reidentified as the unidentified remains found in a coal mine in Fayette County, West Virginia. The remains were later confirmed to be those of the nine Sodder children, who were missing for 67 years. A funeral was held in 2018 to honor the victims.

The case remains one of the most baffling and mysterious in American history, and the reidentification of the Sodder children's remains continues to raise questions about the circumstances of their disappearance.
