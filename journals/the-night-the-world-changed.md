# **The Night the World Changed**

**Country:** United States
**Location:** New York City, New York
**Date:** December 20, 1968
**Status:** Unsolved

## **The Beginning**

It was a dark and stormy night in New York City. The streets were empty, save for a lone taxi driving down 5th Avenue. The taxi's radio crackled to life as the dispatcher spoke in a hushed tone. "We have a situation in the Upper East Side. There's a hostage situation at a bank. The suspect is armed and threatening to harm the hostages."

## **What Happened**

The taxi stopped in front of the bank, which was located on 59th Street and Park Avenue. The passengers exchanged nervous glances as the driver pulled up to the bank's entrance. The suspect, later identified as Richard Matt, a former army medic, stepped out of the taxi and approached the bank's doors.

## **The Investigation**

 NYPD officers and police officers from the FDNY arrived at the scene, surrounding the building and surrounding area. The FBI was also called in, with agents from the Behavioral Science Unit (BSU) arriving to assist in the investigation. The police began to question witnesses, including the bank teller who described the suspect's actions.

## **The Evidence**

A security camera caught footage of the suspect's face, which was later used to identify him. The suspect's fingerprints were found on the bank's door handle, and a eyewitness described a distinctive tattoo on the suspect's hand. The police collected DNA samples from the suspect and the bank's security guards.

## **The Suspects**

Richard Matt was a 38-year-old former army medic who had been struggling with addiction. He had a history of violent behavior and had been involved in several altercations with his ex-wife. The police had previously investigated Matt for a string of burglaries and robberies, but he had always managed to evade capture.

## **The Arrest**

On December 21, 1968, the FBI arrested Richard Matt at his home in New York City. Matt was taken into custody and held without bail until his trial. The police also arrested Robert Burrill, a 35-year-old bank teller who had been held hostage by Matt.

## **The Trial**

Matt was tried in August 1969, but the trial was highly publicized due to the sensational nature of the case. Matt's lawyer, Robert Lawlor, argued that Matt was not guilty by reason of insanity, claiming that Matt had been suffering from a mental illness at the time of the crimes. The prosecution argued that Matt was a hardened killer who had planned and carried out the bank heist.

## **The Aftermath**

The trial was a major media event, with many newspapers and television networks covering the proceedings. Matt was found guilty of first-degree murder and attempted murder, and was sentenced to death. Burrill was also found guilty and sentenced to 30 years to life in prison. The case remains unsolved to this day, with many questions still unanswered.
